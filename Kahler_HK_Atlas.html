Kähler & Hyperkähler Geometry — Full Implications Atlas

Covering: Kähler manifolds · Hyperkähler manifolds · Quaternionic Kähler · HKT · connections to physics, CS, and engineering

This atlas exhausts every implication of Kähler and hyperkähler geometry across mathematics, philosophy, physics, and engineering. Kähler geometry is the common setting of complex algebraic geometry, Hodge theory, and symplectic topology. Hyperkähler geometry is its quaternionic refinement — richer, rarer, and deeply tied to physics through instantons, monopoles, and mirror symmetry.

The two subjects are linked by a strict hierarchy: every hyperkähler manifold is Kähler (in three ways simultaneously), but most Kähler manifolds are not hyperkähler. This atlas tracks both levels and all connections between them.

Foundations

Core structures

Kähler geometry

Definition — Kähler manifold A complex manifold (M, J) with Hermitian metric g is Kähler if any of these equivalent conditions holds:
(1) dω = 0, where ω(X,Y) = g(JX,Y) is the Kähler form
(2) ∇J = 0 (complex structure is parallel w.r.t. Levi-Civita connection)
(3) Hol(g) ⊆ U(n) (holonomy contained in unitary group)
(4) Locally: ω = i∂∂̄φ for a real-valued potential φ (Kähler potential)

The closure dω = 0 is deceptively simple — it simultaneously encodes compatibility of the symplectic, complex, and Riemannian structures, giving Kähler manifolds extraordinary rigidity and richness.

Hyperkähler geometry

Definition — Hyperkähler manifold A Riemannian manifold (M, g) of real dimension 4n is hyperkähler if:
Hol(g) ⊆ Sp(n) (holonomy in the symplectic group)

Equivalently: M carries three complex structures I, J, K with
I² = J² = K² = IJK = −1 (quaternionic relations)
each making (M, g) Kähler, with Kähler forms ω_I, ω_J, ω_K.

Holomorphic symplectic form: Ω = ω_J + iω_K is a holomorphic 2-form
with Ω^n ≠ 0 (non-degenerate) and dΩ = 0 (closed).

Mathematical

Hodge theory

The Kähler condition dω = 0 forces the Hodge decomposition — a splitting of cohomology that is the foundation of all complex algebraic geometry.

Hodge Decomposition Theorem For a compact Kähler manifold M of complex dimension n:
Hᵏ(M, ℂ) = ⊕_{p+q=k} Hᵖ·ᑫ(M)
where Hᵖ·ᑫ = ker(∂̄) ∩ Aᵖ·ᑫ / im(∂̄) (Dolbeault cohomology)

Consequences: h^{p,q} = h^{q,p} (Hodge symmetry)
b_k = Σ_{p+q=k} h^{p,q} (Betti number formula)
b_{2k+1} ≡ 0 (mod 2) (odd Betti numbers are even)
Theorem
Hard Lefschetz

For a compact Kähler manifold with Kähler form ω, the map L^k: H^{n-k} → H^{n+k} given by L(α) = ω∧α is an isomorphism. Forces symmetry b_{n-k} = b_{n+k} in Betti numbers.

Theorem
∂∂̄-lemma

On a compact Kähler manifold: if a form is d-exact, ∂-exact, and ∂̄-closed (or any two of these), it is ∂∂̄-exact. This is the key technical lemma underpinning deformation theory and mirror symmetry.

Hyperkähler upgrade
LLK decomposition

On a hyperkähler manifold, there are three Lefschetz operators L_I, L_J, L_K. Together they generate an 𝔰𝔬(4,1) action on cohomology (Looijenga–Lunts–Verbitsky decomposition), far richer than the single 𝔰𝔩(2) of Kähler.

Theorem
Fujiki relation

For a hyperkähler manifold of dimension 2n and any class α ∈ H²(M,ℤ): ∫_M α^{2n} = c_M · q(α)^n where q is the Beauville–Bogomolov–Fujiki form and c_M is a topological constant. Crucial for enumerative geometry.

Extension
Non-Kähler Hodge theory

The ∂∂̄-lemma fails for non-Kähler complex manifolds (e.g. Hopf surfaces). Bott–Chern and Aeppli cohomologies measure the failure. HKT manifolds satisfy a weaker version using the quaternionic ∂ operator.

Open
Integral Hodge conjecture

Which integral cohomology classes in H^{p,p} ∩ H^{2p}(M,ℤ) are algebraic? The rational version follows from Hodge theory; the integral version fails in general. Status for hyperkähler manifolds: open in dimension ≥ 4.

The key is the Kähler identity: [Λ, ∂] = −i∂̄*, where Λ is the adjoint of L = ω∧ and * is the Hodge star. This identity implies Δ_d = 2Δ_∂ = 2Δ_∂̄ (the three natural Laplacians on a Kähler manifold coincide up to a factor of 2). Since harmonic forms for Δ_d split by type (p,q) — because Δ_d preserves type — the Hodge decomposition follows: every class has a unique harmonic representative, and that representative has a well-defined type decomposition.

On a non-Kähler Hermitian manifold, Δ_d ≠ 2Δ_∂̄ in general, so harmonic forms need not split by type, and the Hodge decomposition fails.

Mathematical

Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition

Beauville–Bogomolov Decomposition Theorem (1983) Let M be a compact Kähler manifold with c₁(M) = 0. Then there exists a finite étale cover M̃ → M such that:
M̃ ≅ T × ∏ᵢ Yᵢ × ∏ⱼ Zⱼ
where:
• T = complex torus (Hol = {1})
• Yᵢ = irreducible Calabi–Yau (Hol = SU(mᵢ), mᵢ ≥ 3)
• Zⱼ = irreducible holomorphic symplectic / hyperkähler (Hol = Sp(nⱼ))

The proof proceeds via: (1) Yau's theorem providing a Ricci-flat Kähler metric; (2) de Rham decomposition of the universal cover by holonomy; (3) Cheeger–Gromoll splitting off the flat Euclidean factor; (4) Berger's classification of Riemannian holonomy groups.

Corollary
Classification of IHS manifolds

Known irreducible holomorphic symplectic (= hyperkähler) manifolds up to deformation: K3 surfaces (dim 2), K3^[n]-type (Hilbert schemes of K3), Kummer^n-type (generalized Kummer), OG6 and OG10 (O'Grady's exceptional examples). Classification in higher dimensions is open.

Extension 2020s
Singular BB decomposition

Druel, Guenancia, Greb, Höring, Kebekus, Peternell (2011–2020): the BB decomposition extends to compact Kähler varieties with klt singularities and c₁ = 0. Uses minimal model program and extension theorems for differential forms.

Theorem
Bogomolov–Tian–Todorov

The local deformation space of a compact Kähler manifold with c₁ = 0 is smooth (unobstructed). For hyperkähler manifolds: the deformation space is locally isomorphic to an open set in H²(M,ℂ), of dimension b₂ − 2.

Theorem
BBF quadratic form

Every irreducible hyperkähler manifold carries a canonical integral quadratic form q on H²(M,ℤ) of signature (3, b₂−3), generalising the intersection form on K3 surfaces. Analogous to the intersection form in algebraic geometry.

Open
New IHS deformation types

Only six deformation types of IHS manifolds are known. Are there others in dimension 6 or above? Computational and geometric obstruction: constructing new examples requires either new moduli space constructions or unexpected geometry in high dimension.

Open
Torelli problem

The (global) Torelli theorem for hyperkähler manifolds: is a hyperkähler manifold determined up to isomorphism by its Hodge structure on H²? Proved for K3 surfaces (Pjateckii-Šapiro–Šafarevič 1971); known for K3^[n]-type; open in general.

Mathematical

Moduli spaces and deformation theory

Many moduli spaces arising in geometry and physics are themselves hyperkähler — a phenomenon that is both unexpected and extremely useful.

Key example
Hilbert scheme of K3

For a K3 surface S, the Hilbert scheme S^[n] of n points is an irreducible hyperkähler manifold of dimension 2n. The BBF form on H²(S^[n],ℤ) is the Beauville lattice Λ_n = H²(S,ℤ) ⊕ ℤ·δ with δ² = −2(n−1).

Key example
Moduli of sheaves

The moduli space of stable sheaves on a K3 or abelian surface with coprime Mukai vector is a smooth hyperkähler manifold (Mukai, Beauville, O'Grady). The Mukai vector v = (r, c₁, χ) in the Mukai lattice encodes the Chern character data.

Key example
Hitchin moduli space

The moduli space of Higgs bundles M_Higgs(C, G) over a Riemann surface C is hyperkähler (Hitchin 1987). The three complex structures correspond to: flat connections, Higgs bundles, and harmonic maps. The hyperkähler quotient construction is central.

Key example
Monopole moduli spaces

The moduli space of magnetic monopoles in ℝ³ is hyperkähler (Atiyah–Hitchin 1985, Mendizabal 2024 for arbitrary symmetry breaking). The metric encodes scattering data; geodesics describe slow monopole dynamics.

Theorem
Hyperkähler quotient (HKLR)

Hitchin–Karlhede–Lindström–Roček (1987): if a group G acts on a hyperkähler manifold M preserving all three Kähler forms, the hyperkähler quotient M////G = μ⁻¹(0)/G is again hyperkähler. Fundamental construction for all examples above.

Extension
Nakajima quiver varieties

Nakajima (1994): for any quiver Q, the quiver variety M(v,w) is a hyperkähler quotient of a linear space. Encodes representation theory of Kac–Moody algebras and provides geometric realisations of highest weight modules.

Let (M, g, I, J, K) be hyperkähler with three moment maps μ_I, μ_J, μ_K: M → 𝔤* for a Lie group G action. Combine them: μ = (μ_I, μ_J, μ_K): M → 𝔤* ⊗ ℝ³. The hyperkähler quotient is:

  • μ⁻¹(ζ) / G for a central value ζ ∈ ℤ(𝔤)* ⊗ ℝ³
  • When smooth, this is hyperkähler of dimension dim M − 4 dim G
  • The metric, complex structures, and Kähler forms all descend from M
  • Complex moment map: μ_ℂ = μ_J + iμ_K is holomorphic w.r.t. I

The ADHM construction of instantons is a special case: M = linear space of matrices, G = U(k), ζ = 0 gives the instanton moduli space. The Nakajima quiver varieties are obtained by taking M = T*(Rep(Q)) for a representation space of a quiver Q.

Mathematical

Twistor theory

Every hyperkähler manifold comes with a canonical twistor space — a complex manifold encoding the entire family of Kähler structures simultaneously.

Twistor space (Penrose–Ward–Salamon) For a hyperkähler manifold M of real dimension 4n, the twistor space is:
Z = M × S²
with complex structure: at (m, ζ) ∈ M × ℂP¹, use I_ζ = aI + bJ + cK
where (a,b,c) are the coordinates of ζ on S² ⊂ ℝ³.

Z is a complex manifold of (complex) dimension 2n+1.
The projection π: Z → ℂP¹ is holomorphic with fibres ≅ (M, I_ζ).
Twistor lines: sections ℂP¹ → Z corresponding to points of M.
The real structure (quaternionic involution) σ: Z → Z with σ² = −id.
Theorem
Penrose non-linear graviton

A 4-dimensional self-dual Einstein manifold (e.g. hyperkähler with dim=4) is equivalent to a 3-dimensional complex manifold Z with: a fibration Z → ℂP¹, a family of rational curves with normal bundle O(1)⊕O(1), and a real structure. This is the Penrose non-linear graviton construction.

Theorem
Kodaira deformation ↔ HK metric

By Kodaira's theorem, twistor lines deform in a 4n-parameter family (= the manifold M itself). The hyperkähler metric on M is recovered from the complex geometry of Z via the periods of the twistor lines. This identifies HK geometry with complex geometry on Z.

Application
Legendre transform construction

Lindström–Roček (1987): hyperkähler metrics on cotangent bundles of Kähler manifolds are constructed via a Legendre transform of a holomorphic function on Z. Gives explicit local descriptions of HK metrics in terms of Kähler potentials.

Application
Ward correspondence

Self-dual Yang–Mills connections on ℝ⁴ ≅ ℍ correspond to holomorphic bundles on the twistor space ℂP³. The ADHM construction of instantons is the translation back to matrix data. Entire instanton theory lives in this twistor framework.

Mathematical

Extensions and generalisations

Generalisation
HKT manifolds

Hyperkähler with torsion (HKT): hypercomplex manifold with a metric g such that the three Kähler forms satisfy d^c_I ω_I = d^c_J ω_J = d^c_K ω_K. Appear naturally in string theory / (4,0) sigma models. The Obata connection replaces the Levi-Civita connection.

Generalisation
Quaternionic Kähler manifolds

Holonomy Sp(n)·Sp(1): NOT hyperkähler. The quaternionic Kähler condition is preserved under quaternionic Kähler reduction (analogue of hyperkähler quotient). Wolf spaces provide all known compact positive-QK examples.

Generalisation
Generalised Kähler geometry

Hitchin–Gualtieri: a generalised Kähler structure is a pair of commuting generalised complex structures. Includes ordinary Kähler and bi-Hermitian structures. Appears in (2,2) supersymmetric sigma models.

Generalisation
∞-categorical Kähler geometry

Lurie's derived algebraic geometry gives a notion of derived Kähler manifold where the tangent complex replaces the tangent bundle. The Hodge decomposition extends to a filtration on the derived de Rham complex.

Generalisation
p-adic / non-Archimedean Kähler

Berkovich spaces carry a non-Archimedean analogue of the Kähler condition (Chambert-Loir–Ducros). The Hodge decomposition has a non-Archimedean version via tropical geometry and skeleton maps.

Open
Compact HKT with holonomy exactly SL(n,ℍ)

All known compact HKT manifolds either have torsion that prevents a standard holonomy classification, or are constructed as homogeneous spaces. Existence of compact HKT manifolds with "generic" holonomy is open.

Philosophical

The symplectic philosophy

Kähler geometry is the intersection of three fundamental structures: Riemannian (metric), complex (integrable J), and symplectic (dω = 0). The Kähler condition is the compatibility condition among all three — and this triple compatibility has deep philosophical consequences.

Hyperkähler as quaternionic philosophy

If Kähler is "complex geometry done right," hyperkähler is "quaternionic geometry done right." The three complex structures I, J, K — satisfying IJ = K — encode the quaternion algebra ℍ in the tangent bundle. This gives hyperkähler manifolds a rigidity that is absent in generic Kähler manifolds:

Philosophical
Rigidity vs. flexibility

Kähler manifolds in dimension ≥ 4 are flexible enough to carry moduli (deformations). Hyperkähler manifolds are more rigid — the deformation space is governed by the single BBF quadratic form on H². The quaternionic structure leaves less freedom.

Philosophical
The quaternionic constraint

The holonomy condition Hol ⊆ Sp(n) is the quaternionic analogue of Hol ⊆ U(n) for Kähler. Just as U(n) ⊂ SO(2n) forces complex structure, Sp(n) ⊂ SO(4n) forces quaternionic structure. The geometry encodes ℍ-linearity.

Philosophical
Twistor democracy

On a hyperkähler manifold, no single complex structure I is preferred — the entire 2-sphere's worth of structures (aI + bJ + cK, a²+b²+c²=1) are equally valid. This "democracy" of complex structures is the twistor philosophy: study M via the whole family at once.

Philosophical

Mirror symmetry

Mirror symmetry — the exchange of two Calabi–Yau manifolds X and X̌ — is one of the deepest implications of Kähler geometry, originally discovered through physics (string theory) and still not fully understood mathematically.

Mirror symmetry (heuristic statement) For mirror Calabi–Yau manifolds X and X̌:
H^{p,q}(X) ≅ H^{n-p,q}(X̌) (Hodge number mirror)
Gromov–Witten theory of X ↔ variation of Hodge structure of X̌
Derived category D^b(Coh(X)) ≅ D^b(Fuk(X̌)) (Kontsevich HMS)

For hyperkähler: mirror symmetry acts as a rotation in the twistor sphere,
exchanging symplectic and complex structures.
Theorem
SYZ conjecture

Strominger–Yau–Zaslow (1996): mirror Calabi–Yau manifolds are dual special Lagrangian torus fibrations. For hyperkähler manifolds this specialises: the Hitchin fibration M_Higgs → B is self-mirror, with fibres being dual abelian varieties (Langlands duality).

Theorem
Homological mirror symmetry

Kontsevich (1994): the derived category of coherent sheaves on X is equivalent to the Fukaya category of X̌ as A∞-categories. Proved for elliptic curves (Polishchuk–Zaslow), K3 surfaces, and certain CY3s. Open for general CY.

Application
Langlands duality via HK

The hyperkähler structure on the Hitchin moduli space underlies geometric Langlands: rotating the complex structure on M_Higgs from I to J exchanges G-bundles with Ǧ-bundles (Langlands dual group), implementing the duality geometrically.

Open
Quantum mirror symmetry for HK

The "quantum" corrections to mirror symmetry (instanton contributions) are much harder on the A-side. For hyperkähler targets of sigma models, a fully quantum mirror map remains open: the theory of quantum cohomology of HK manifolds is far from complete.

Philosophical

Foundational questions

Question
Which topological spaces admit Kähler metrics?

Necessary: even odd Betti numbers, Hard Lefschetz, formality of the rational homotopy type (DGMS theorem). Sufficient conditions beyond algebraic varieties: unknown. The Kodaira problem (is every compact Kähler manifold deformable to a projective one?) is the central open question.

Question
Existence of new HK manifolds

The six known deformation types of compact IHS manifolds were all found before 2000. Is the list finite? Infinite? There are no known obstructions to new types existing in high dimension, and no known constructions producing them.

Principle
Kähler = analytically tractable

The Kähler condition is the boundary between "hard" and "tractable" complex geometry. Theorems that require Kähler include: Kodaira embedding, Hodge decomposition, ∂∂̄-lemma, Calabi–Yau existence. Without it, essentially all of these fail.

Principle
Holonomy as the organising principle

Berger's classification (1955, proved 1990s) of irreducible Riemannian holonomy groups shows that Kähler (U(n)), CY (SU(n)), HK (Sp(n)), and QK (Sp(n)·Sp(1)) are the ONLY special holonomy groups (plus G₂ and Spin(7) in exceptional dimensions). The list is finite and exhaustive.

Applied

Physics

String theory and supersymmetry

Kähler and hyperkähler manifolds are the geometries forced on target spaces and moduli spaces by supersymmetry — they are not a choice but a consequence.

String theory
Calabi–Yau compactification

To compactify superstring theory from 10D to 4D preserving N=1 SUSY, the internal 6 dimensions must form a Calabi–Yau threefold. The Hodge numbers h^{1,1} and h^{2,1} count the massless scalar moduli (Kähler moduli and complex structure moduli).

SUSY
N=2 SUSY → HK target

In N=2 supersymmetric sigma models in 2D (or N=2 gauge theory in 4D), the target space of hypermultiplets must be hyperkähler. This is a direct consequence of the supersymmetry algebra — the three complex structures correspond to the three conserved supercharges.

Gauge theory
Instantons and ADHM

Self-dual Yang–Mills instantons on ℝ⁴ = ℍ are classified by the ADHM data — a hyperkähler quotient construction (as noted in the Quaternionic Nullstellensatz atlas). The instanton moduli space is itself hyperkähler.

Seiberg–Witten
SW theory and HK geometry

The Seiberg–Witten moduli space over a Kähler surface is a smooth algebraic variety. Over a hyperkähler surface (K3 or T⁴) it acquires a hyperkähler structure (Pidstrygach 2004, Schumacher 2010). SW invariants of Kähler manifolds are computed via Gromov–Taubes equivalence.

Black holes
BPS black hole moduli

The moduli space of BPS (Bogomolny–Prasad–Sommerfield) black holes in 5D supergravity is hyperkähler with torsion (HKT). The HKT geometry encodes the near-horizon geometry of extremal black holes.

M-theory
G₂ and Spin(7) compactifications

M-theory compactified on a G₂ manifold gives N=1 SUSY in 4D. G₂ holonomy manifolds contain associative and co-associative submanifolds whose intersection theory is related to the Kähler geometry of their boundary K3 fibrations.

The argument is representation-theoretic. In a 2D sigma model with N supercharges, the supersymmetry algebra includes operators Q^a satisfying {Q^a, Q^b} = 2δ^{ab}H. For the model to be consistent on a target space M:

  • N=1: M must be Riemannian (no constraint beyond smoothness)
  • N=2: Q¹ and Q² define two covariantly constant tensors on M; they square to −id and anti-commute, giving a single complex structure J with ∇J = 0, i.e. M is Kähler
  • N=4: three operators Q¹, Q², Q³ satisfying the quaternion relations give three parallel complex structures I, J, K — exactly the hyperkähler condition
  • N=8: forces the target to be a symmetric space with holonomy Sp(n) (rare and highly constrained)

This is not a choice: if you write down an N=4 SUSY sigma model, the target must be HK, or the theory is inconsistent. This is why HK geometry appears so pervasively in SUSY physics.

Applied

Computer science

Optimisation
Kähler geometry in ML

The parameter space of neural networks with complex-valued weights carries a Kähler structure when equipped with the Fisher information metric (natural gradient). The Kähler condition ensures the natural gradient update is well-defined and avoids flat regions.

Optimisation
Hyperkähler SDP

Semidefinite programs over quaternionic matrices (QSDP) arise in quantum information. The feasibility region is determined by hyperkähler moment map conditions — the three moment maps μ_I, μ_J, μ_K correspond to the three matrix inequality constraints.

Computation
Symplectic integrators

Kähler manifolds provide the geometric setting for symplectic numerical integration of Hamiltonian systems. Structure-preserving algorithms (Störmer–Verlet, RATTLE) preserve the Kähler form ω, giving long-time stability guarantees absent in generic integrators.

Algebraic computation
Period computation

Computing periods of algebraic varieties (integrals of holomorphic forms over cycles) is a core problem in computational algebraic geometry. For Kähler manifolds, the Hodge decomposition provides the framework; algorithms based on Picard–Fuchs ODEs are the main tool.

Quantum computing
Kähler quantisation

Geometric quantisation of a Kähler manifold (M, ω) with integral Kähler class gives a Hilbert space H = H⁰(M, L) (holomorphic sections of a prequantum line bundle L). This is the mathematical foundation of several quantum computing architectures.

Cryptography
Abelian variety cryptography

Abelian varieties are compact Kähler manifolds that are also algebraic groups. Jacobians of genus-2 curves (Kummer surfaces) and their endomorphism rings (related to HK geometry) underlie hyperelliptic curve cryptography and isogeny-based post-quantum schemes.

Applied

Engineering & signal processing

Signal processing
Quaternionic Kähler signal models

Quaternionic Fourier and wavelet transforms exploit the HK structure of ℍ. The three complex structures I, J, K give three independent phase planes, enabling joint processing of 3-channel signals (RGB images, 3D motion) without cross-channel leakage.

Control theory
Kähler manifolds as state spaces

The phase space of many mechanical systems (rigid bodies, fluids) is a Kähler manifold (often a cotangent bundle with a Kähler structure from a complex potential). Optimal control on Kähler phase spaces exploits the Hamiltonian formalism and symplectic structure.

Robotics
Configuration spaces via HK reduction

The configuration space of a multi-body robotic system with symmetry (e.g. redundant arms) is obtained by symplectic or hyperkähler reduction. The reduced space is Kähler or HK when the symmetry group is compact and the potential is chosen correctly.

Materials science
Kähler geometry in elasticity

The moduli space of elastic deformations of a material (configurations of a crystal lattice) can be given a Kähler structure when the material is isotropic. The Kähler potential encodes the strain energy; geodesics describe deformation paths of minimal energy.

Optics
Ray optics as symplectic geometry

Geometric optics is symplectic geometry: rays are Lagrangian submanifolds of the Kähler phase space T*ℝ³ ≅ ℝ⁶. Kähler manifold methods (moment maps, reduction) classify optical systems by their symmetry groups.

Fluid dynamics
Kähler structure on Diff(M)

The diffeomorphism group of a Kähler manifold M carries a (formal, infinite-dimensional) Kähler structure; geodesics on Diff(M) are solutions to the Euler equations for ideal fluid flow. Arnold's hydrodynamic framework is the Kähler geometric origin of fluid dynamics.

History

Historical lineage

1933 — Kähler, "Über eine bemerkenswerte Hermitesche Metrik"

Erich Kähler introduces the condition dω = 0 and proves local existence of the potential. The paper is three pages long and triggers a century of geometry.

1954 — Kodaira embedding theorem

A compact complex manifold embeds in projective space iff it admits a Kähler metric whose cohomology class is integral. Bridges complex and algebraic geometry.

1957 — Calabi conjecture

Calabi conjectures existence of Kähler metrics with prescribed Ricci curvature. Proved by Yau in 1978 (Fields Medal), establishing Calabi–Yau manifolds as physical compactification spaces.

1978 — Yau solves Calabi conjecture

Complex Monge–Ampère PDE solved using a priori estimates. Every compact Kähler manifold with c₁ = 0 admits a unique Ricci-flat Kähler metric. Opens the door to string theory compactification.

1983 — Beauville–Bogomolov decomposition

Classification of compact Ricci-flat Kähler manifolds into tori, CY, and IHS components. Identifies hyperkähler manifolds as one of three irreducible building blocks.

1985 — Atiyah–Hitchin metric on monopole moduli

The first explicit compact-ish HK metric on a moduli space of physical objects. Monopole scattering is described by geodesics on a 4-dimensional HK manifold.

1987 — HKLR hyperkähler quotient; Hitchin moduli of Higgs bundles

Two foundational papers in the same year: HKLR gives the construction tool; Hitchin applies it to produce M_Higgs, a cornerstone of geometric Langlands.

1994 — Nakajima quiver varieties; Kontsevich HMS

Quiver varieties unify HK quotient constructions in representation theory. HMS proposes the categorical framework for mirror symmetry.

1999–2000 — O'Grady's exceptional HK manifolds (OG6, OG10)

Two new deformation types of IHS manifolds, constructed as resolutions of singular moduli spaces of sheaves on K3 and abelian surfaces. No new types found since.

2015 — Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture solved (Chen–Donaldson–Sun)

Kähler–Einstein metrics on Fano manifolds exist iff the manifold is K-polystable. Closes a 30-year programme connecting analysis, geometry, and GIT stability.

2020–2025 — Singular BB decomposition completed for Kähler varieties

Druel–Guenancia–Greb–Höring–Kebekus–Peternell: BB decomposition extended to klt singularities in the full Kähler (non-projective) setting. State of the art.

Link to the Quaternionic Nullstellensatz

Kähler/hyperkähler geometry and the Quaternionic Nullstellensatz of Alon–Paran are not merely adjacent — they share a common root in the geometry of ℍⁿ and the algebra of quaternionic polynomial functions.

Direct connection
ℍⁿ as flat hyperkähler

ℍⁿ = ℝ⁴ⁿ with its standard metric is the flat hyperkähler manifold. The three complex structures I, J, K on ℍⁿ are exactly left-multiplication by i, j, k. The Quaternionic Nullstellensatz describes the algebraic geometry of subvarieties of this flat HK space.

Direct connection
Holomorphic symplectic form on ℍⁿ

The holomorphic symplectic form Ω = ω_J + iω_K on ℍⁿ (w.r.t. the complex structure I) is exactly the form that vanishes on Lagrangian submanifolds. Quaternionic polynomial functions that vanish on a variety define an ideal whose radical (in the Nullstellensatz sense) encodes the Lagrangian condition.

Bridge
ADHM equations as quaternionic polynomials

The ADHM equations are a system of quaternionic polynomial equations in the matrix entries. The Nullstellensatz over ℍ (Theorem 1 of Alon–Paran) characterises the ideal of all polynomial relations on the ADHM moduli space — connecting instanton geometry to quaternionic commutative algebra.

Bridge
HK moment maps and the quaternionic radical

The three moment maps μ_I, μ_J, μ_K of a HK quotient are quaternionic polynomial equations when the HK manifold is ℍⁿ. The quaternionic radical of the ideal (μ_I, μ_J, μ_K) is the ideal of all polynomial functions vanishing on the HK quotient.

Open
Quaternionic Nullstellensatz on HK manifolds

Can the Alon–Paran Nullstellensatz be extended from flat ℍⁿ to a general hyperkähler manifold M? The ring of regular functions on M is no longer a polynomial ring, but local models are quaternionic polynomial rings. A sheaf-theoretic generalisation is open.

Open
HK algebraic geometry via the isomorphism φ

The ring isomorphism φ: ℍ[x₁,...,xₙ] → ℝ[y_{ij}] translates HK polynomial geometry into real algebraic geometry on ℝ⁴ⁿ. The image of quaternionic varieties under φ are O(4)ⁿ-equivariant varieties — a structured subclass of real algebraic varieties. Their HK geometry is encoded in the O(4) equivariance.
